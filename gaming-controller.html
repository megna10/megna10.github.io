Adaptive Gaming Controller

MIT Assistive Technology Club · Feb. 2026 – Present

Overview

Co-designed and engineered an accessible gaming hardware interface tailored for a user with limited arm and finger mobility. The project bridges physical ergonomic inputs — including custom footpedals, reshaped gamepads, and digital joystick emulation — with a Sony PS5 DualSense controller to restore full gaming capability.

Hardware Architecture & Engineering Highlights

  • KiCad Schematic & Electronics Design: Engineered a central control board around an ESP32-S3 microcontroller to manage low-latency input acquisition and signal translation.
  • Digital Joystick Emulation: Integrated MCP4251 digital potentiometers via SPI to dynamically translate custom physical joystick and pedal inputs into native analog voltages recognized by the console.
  • Motion & Auxiliary Sensing: Embedded a BNO055 9-DOF IMU via I²C to convert physical footpedal rotation into tilt-assisted control axes.
  • Audio & Feedback Pipeline: Incorporated a MAX98357A I²S audio amplifier driving an onboard speaker, and a SPH0645LM4H MEMS microphone for voice-assisted commands and audio cues.
  • Console Hardware Interfacing: Reverse-engineered stock PS5 DualSense controller PCB signal lines to solder and map external microswitches, digital pots, and toggle controls directly onto the console's native PCB.

Technical Stack & Tools

  • EDA & PCB Design: KiCad (schematic capture, component selection, signal routing)
  • Embedded Hardware: ESP32-S3, MCP4251, BNO055, MAX98357A, SPH0645LM4H
  • Communication Protocols: SPI, I²C, I²S, GPIO hardware interrupts
  • Prototyping & Tools: DualSense signal mapping, oscilloscope signal verification, breadboard validation
